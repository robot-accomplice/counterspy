# Phase 2.5 — Streaming messages, and merging Intercept into Exfiltration

**Status:** REVISION 3 after the second adversarial round (`cp-spec2.5-r2`, 2026-07-17). Round 2 found 14
issues (two independent reviewers + orchestrator, cross-confirmed); this revision resolves them. Round 1
had invalidated §3.1's original mechanism (independent-direction parsing) and found the live secret leak
(fixed `419d421`). Maintainer decisions of 2026-07-17 are folded in and marked **[D1]–[D5]**.
**Predecessor:** `2026-07-15-phase2-tls-interception-design.md` (shipped; verified live 2026-07-16).

## 0. What this revision changed

Round 2's fatal four, and where each is fixed:

- **A. The merge's join key did not exist.** Egress rows for a proxied app are keyed by the *loopback*
  proxy endpoint (`127.0.0.1:62443`), intercept messages by the *real* destination — "messages for that
  app + destination" could never match, and counterspy's own relay traffic topped the rollup
  double-counted. Fixed in §3.2 (proxy-endpoint recognition, app-keyed join, self-exclusion).
- **B. The correlation queue wedged on a legal early response.** "Push as each request completes" +
  RFC 7230 early final response + client abort ⇒ no queue entry ever. Fixed in §3.1.1: push at request
  **head** completion, empty-pop = desync, queue bounded.
- **C. The encouraged daemon was unreadable.** The plist runs with no `--stream` (no socket exists), and
  the log is 0600 root in a 0700 root dir — the merged non-root console could read neither, and the
  "honest" empty state would have lied permanently. Fixed by **[D1]** (§3.3 ownership model, daemon gains
  `--stream`) and **[D2]** (§3.2 log fallback; §6.1 corrected — `ReadLog` survives).
- **D. The `streaming`/`partial` state machine contradicted itself** (§3.1.5 vs §7 vs §3.1.6, and §6.3's
  no-heartbeat rule). Fixed in §3.1.5: a full event state machine — at most two events per message, ever.
- Plus the ten singles: loud-rejection destination (§3.1.7), fabricated textproto claim (§3.1.5),
  h2 measurement (**[D4]**, §2/§9), brotli decision (**[D3]**, §2), close-mid-message (§3.1.8), dangling
  `console --intercept` pointers (§6.1), `opaque` needs a reason (§3.1.6 `Reason`), trailers (§3.1.2),
  socket-drop visibility (§3.2), sub-second ordering (§3.1.6).

## 1. Why

Phase 2 works — it decrypts real traffic, attributes it to apps, and reverts cleanly. Live use and
review exposed four faults.

**1.1 The capture is truncated and the UI implied it wasn't.** The proxy keeps `FlowCaptureBytes` (8 KB)
per direction in a `capBuf` and publishes one record at connection close. A real session captured a
26,836-byte Sentry POST from Proton Mail — ~70% never recorded. The view then capped the *display* at 8
lines and printed `… (5 more lines)`, implying content that did not exist. The display cap is fixed
(`9952a6c`); the **capture** cap remains, and buffer-then-truncate is the wrong shape.

**1.2 Flows are stamped at open, published at close.** Keep-alive connections surface after shorter ones
that started later (`14:44:58` printed above `14:44:28`). The viewer sorts by `At` to compensate — a
workaround for a record shape that does not match reality. (Round 2 addendum: `At` is *seconds*
precision, so even the workaround has no total order across a browser's sub-second burst — see §3.1.6.)

**1.3 Intercepted and Exfiltration are the same thing.** Exfiltration asks *"this app is trusted; what is
it sending?"* and its inspector answers with content until it hits `ENCRYPTED (TLS) — metadata only`.
Intercept removes that wall; it does not ask a new question. The split produced a duplicate per-app
rollup beside a per-app rollup, and the Intercepted view was rebuilt four times because the split was
accepted rather than questioned.

**1.4 Bounded capture defeated masking (FIXED, `419d421`).** `reBodySecret`'s value alternation required
a closing quote, so a body cut mid-value (`"api_key":"sk_live_ABC`) matched neither branch and published
the partial secret **in the clear**. Reproduced against the live pipeline. Fixed by adding the dangling
open-quote case, mirroring `rePEMOpen`. **This is a precondition for §6.2**: any design that publishes
partial text must assume truncation lands mid-token, and the masking rules must survive it.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Per-message streaming

The relay stays a **byte-for-byte mirror**: bytes are forwarded verbatim, and a *copy* is parsed. We do
NOT become a re-serialising HTTP proxy — that would change what the server sees and break the read-only
invariant. (One shutdown-timing exception, honestly recorded: §3.1.8.)

**3.1.1 Framing requires request↔response correlation.** The two parse goroutines share a **request
queue** with exact semantics, pinned here because round 2 proved "as each request completes" is fatal:

- The request parser pushes `{method, seq}` when the request **head** completes (request line + headers),
  *not* when the body finishes. The head carries the only framing fact the response parser needs (the
  method); body completion is irrelevant to response framing and must not gate it.
- The response parser pops one entry to frame each final response (`HEAD` → no body regardless of
  `Content-Length`; `204`/`304` → no body; `1xx` informational → frame, publish nothing, do **not** pop).
- **Empty-pop is a desync.** A response head arriving with an empty queue means framing is lost
  (or the tunnel began mid-conversation) → §3.1.3, `opaque` once.
- **The queue is bounded** (`MaxPipelinedRequests`, proposal 64 — browsers pipeline ≤6). Exceeding it is
  pathological enough that honesty beats cleverness → §3.1.3, `opaque` once. Entries are never reaped by
  age; a request that never gets a response leaves its entry until connection end (64 × ~40 bytes —
  the bound is what matters, not the reclaim).
- An RFC 7230 **early final response** (server rejects `Expect: 100-continue`, early 401/413) now frames
  correctly: the entry was pushed at head completion, so the response publishes normally; the aborted
  request body is §3.1.8's close/abort case, not a desync.

**3.1.2 The pump needs its OWN framing reader.** `inspect.readBody` must **not** be reused: it is a
one-shot *preview* reader (`io.ReadAll(io.LimitReader(body, maxRawBody))` then `Close()`) that never
drains the remainder — reusing it guarantees permanent desync. The pump's reader separates *how much to
keep* (bounded, for display) from *how much to skip* (all of it, to stay framed):

- `Content-Length`: keep min(N, cap), skip the rest by count.
- `chunked`: walk chunk headers, keep bounded payload, skip the rest — **and consume the trailer-part**:
  after the terminal `0` chunk, read trailer fields until the blank line (RFC 7230 §4.1.2), whether or
  not `Trailer` was declared. S3 `aws-chunked` checksums and gRPC-web trailers are legitimate and common
  in exactly the traffic we watch; a walker that stops at `0\r\n\r\n` mis-frames the *next* message.

This is what makes §6.2's "publish a partial and keep framing" achievable at all.

**3.1.3 Desync ⇒ opaque, once.** Any framing failure — an unparseable message, a protocol upgrade
(`101`), an empty-pop or queue overflow (§3.1.1), header block over the cap (§3.1.5), anything that stops
being HTTP/1.1 — resolves to §6.2's second branch: mark the connection `opaque` **once** (§6.3), with
`Reason` set (§3.1.6), stop parsing, keep relaying. There is no "resync after garbage" path; attempting
one would publish fabricated messages, which is worse than admitting we lost framing.

**3.1.4 Events are PER DIRECTION, not paired.** A paired request+response event must wait for the
response, which (a) delays showing a request whose response is slow or never arrives, and (b)
re-introduces §1.2's ordering bug at message granularity when a response completes before a
slow-uploading request. Instead each direction publishes when **it** completes, carrying `ConnID`+`Seq`
so the viewer groups them for display. A request that never gets a response still appears — honestly, as
a request with no response. (Approved: **[D5]**.)

**3.1.5 Bounds, and the per-message event state machine.** Per-message is not unbounded, and round 2
showed the *states* must be pinned, not just the numbers:

- `MaxMessageBytes` (proposal: 1 MB) and `MaxMessageAge` (proposal: 30s) bound one message.
- **One message produces at most two events, ever:**
  1. At most one **`streaming`** snapshot — fired exactly once, if `MaxMessageAge` elapses before the
     message completes. Never repeats: a never-ending SSE/long-poll/LLM stream yields one snapshot and
     then silence until it ends. This is §6.3's no-heartbeat rule applied to *decrypted* connections —
     round 2 caught that the ban covered only `opaque` ones.
  2. Exactly one **terminal** event: **`complete`** (fully captured) or **`partial`** — "this is all you
     will ever get" — published when `MaxMessageBytes` is hit (skip path continues framing), or when the
     connection closes/errors mid-message (§3.1.8), or at normal completion of an over-cap body.
     `partial` always carries `Reason` (`size cap`, `connection closed`, …).
- A message that fired `streaming` and later completes still gets its terminal event. The viewer
  *replaces* a `streaming` entry with its terminal successor (same `ConnID`+`Seq`+`Direction`) — never
  appends a duplicate.
- **The header block is bounded by us, full stop.** Round 2 struck the claim that `net/textproto`
  applies its own ceiling — it does not (`ReadMIMEHeader` is unlimited; `DefaultMaxHeaderBytes` exists
  only inside `http.Server`). The pump imposes `MaxHeaderBytes` (proposal: 64 KB) per header block;
  over-cap → §3.1.3. The same bound is applied to the proxy's existing CONNECT read
  (`proxy.go:70-72`), which today accepts unbounded headers from any local client (ticket T-20) —
  folded into this phase because it is the same mechanism in the same file.
- **Decoded size is already bounded** and must stay so: `decodeBody`'s `io.LimitReader(r, maxBodyPreview+1)`
  caps *decoded* output regardless of input, so compression bombs are handled today. Do not regress this.

**3.1.6 Model shape.**

```
InterceptedMessage {
  SchemaVersion int     // REQUIRED — see 3.1.7
  ConnID   string  // proxy-process-unique connection id; groups all events of one connection
  Seq      int     // exchange sequence within the connection; 0 for connection-level events
  Direction string  // request | response; EMPTY for connection-level events
  At       string  // RFC3339Nano, when THIS event completed — nano precision + (ConnID,Seq) tiebreak
                   // gives the viewer a total order; seconds-precision re-creates §1.2 sub-second
  PID      int
  App      string
  DestName, DestIP, SNI string
  Status   string  // decrypted | pinned | opaque | error (closed set, unchanged)
  Text     string  // decoded + masked; "" for non-decrypted statuses
  Bytes    int     // wire bytes observed for this message AT PUBLISH TIME
  Total    int     // expected total when known (Content-Length), else 0 — "kept 1.0 MB of 26.8 MB"
  State    string  // complete | partial | streaming — terminal = complete|partial (see 3.1.5)
  Reason   string  // why: partial cause / opaque cause / error / pinned detail; "" when unremarkable
}
```

- `State` distinguishes two facts a reader must not conflate: **`partial`** = all you will ever get;
  **`streaming`** = a bounded snapshot, terminal event follows.
- `pinned` / `opaque` / `error` remain **per-connection** facts published exactly once (§6.3), with
  `Seq: 0`, empty `Direction`/`Text`, and `Reason` **always set** — today the reason is recorded
  (`DestName: "(unresolved: …)"`); dropping it would make a desync epidemic un-root-causable (Rule 14).
- `Bytes` is honestly *observed-so-far*: for a capped/closed `partial` the true total may be unknowable
  (chunked, or close before `Content-Length` bytes arrive). `Total` carries the expectation when the
  peer declared one; no correction events are published later.

**3.1.7 The wire format must be versioned — and "loud" has an address.** `json.Unmarshal` silently
ignores unknown fields and leaves missing ones zero — so an OLD log record read by NEW code parses
without error into an *empty but apparently complete* `decrypted` message. That is the §1.1 sin served
by the reader instead of the writer. `SchemaVersion` is required, and rejection is loud **through the
event stream, not around it**:

- The one ingestion function (§3.2) checks `SchemaVersion` *before* best-effort parsing. A record with a
  missing or newer version is **not parsed**; the reader synthesises exactly one `error` event per
  stream — `Reason: "unsupported record version (got X, want 2) — is the daemon the same build?"` — and
  counts further rejections, surfaced as a number in the inspector chrome (`3 records rejected`).
- Malformed-JSON lines: counted and summarised the same way, never one-notice-per-line. Per-record
  loudness through an untrusted socket is a spam weapon; per-stream notice + counter is loud without
  being weaponisable. The feed is never aborted over a bad record.
- Socket and log carry the **same** record schema **[D2]**; both readers share this path.

**3.1.8 Close, half-close, and abort are first-class.** Round 1 specified completions; round 2 found the
terminations unspecified. Rules:

- The relay's current behaviour — closing **both** conns on the **first** direction's EOF
  (`proxy.go:265-268`) — changes: each direction ends independently. A client `SHUT_WR` after its
  request must not truncate an in-flight response; the upstream→client copy continues until its own
  EOF/error/idle. A write error ends only that direction. Both done ⇒ close. This is a behavioural
  change to the mirror (still byte-verbatim forwarding; only shutdown timing) and is recorded here as
  the one exception to "relay unchanged."
- A connection that ends mid-message publishes the in-flight message as terminal **`partial`** with
  `Reason: "connection closed"` (or the error). No abort state is added — `partial` already means "all
  you will ever get" — but close **is** now an explicit `partial` trigger (round 2: it was not listed,
  so the 95%-arrived message would have vanished, §1.1's dishonesty at the boundary).
- The 5-minute idle reap is just one cause of "connection closed" and follows the same rule.

### 3.2 Merge into Exfiltration

**The merge point is the inspector's verdict, not a tab.** With a live daemon, `ENCRYPTED (TLS) —
metadata only` gains an answer. Round 2 proved the naive join cannot work; the join below is the design.

- **Proxy-endpoint recognition.** The console knows the intercept proxy endpoint
  (`127.0.0.1:interceptProxyPort`, a build constant). An egress connection leaf to that endpoint is a
  **proxied** connection — its displayed destination is taken from the intercept stream, never from
  nettop (which can only see the loopback peer).
- **The join is app-keyed.** The inspector's per-app message buffer is keyed by `App` (+`PID`); inspect
  (`i`) on a proxied app's row renders that app's intercept messages (decrypted, masked, time-ordered
  by the §3.1.6 total order, with dividers, honest non-decrypted reasons and truncation notices already
  built) — because every proxied connection of an app shares the one loopback endpoint, destination
  scoping comes from the *messages* (`DestName`/`DestIP`), not from the row.
- **Self-exclusion (required).** counterspy's own process is excluded from the Exfiltration rollup: the
  root proxy's upstream dials carry *other apps'* bytes, so counting them double-counts every proxied
  byte and would feed `Concern()` scoring until the tool flags itself as the top exfil candidate. A
  footnote line states the exclusion honestly (`counterspy's relay traffic excluded — it carries other
  apps' bytes`).
- **Exfiltration rows otherwise unchanged** (nettop/lsof rollup per app) — unproxied traffic keeps the
  existing axis and the existing bpf verdict.
- **Log fallback [D1][D2].** Entering Exfiltration with a daemon installed: read the bounded log tail
  (through the one ingestion point) for captures made while no console ran — the daemon's entire
  justification — then follow the live socket. No live socket: log only. This is why `publish.ReadLog`
  is *not* deleted (§6.1, corrected).
- **Honest empty states — three, never one lie:**
  1. No daemon installed: `No live intercept daemon found. Showing only unencrypted traffic.`
  2. Daemon installed but artifacts unreadable (EACCES): `intercept daemon is installed but its log is
     not readable by this user — ownership repair: sudo counterspy intercept --repair-daemon` (or the
     documented manual step; never report EACCES as absence).
  3. Daemon installed and readable, nothing captured yet: `daemon running — no intercepted messages yet.`
- **Sudo prominence:** make the existing startup warning prominent; re-state on entering Exfiltration:
  `No traffic found — is the intercept daemon running as sudo?`
- **Single ingestion point (required).** The per-app message buffer the inspector queries MUST be fed
  *only* through the sanitise-gated read path (`ReadSocket`/`ReadLog` → `scanMessages` →
  `sanitizeMessage`). The socket is untrusted input (anyone who can write it). `sanitizeMessage`
  (rewritten from `sanitizeFlow`) caps `App` (closing §6a.3), caps text fields, coerces unknown
  `Status`/`State`, and performs §3.1.7's version gate. The plan names this one function.
- **Drop and eviction honesty.** The socket→view channel is bounded; a slow console drops events and the
  drop counter is rendered (`N events dropped — console too slow`) — a dropped response event must never
  masquerade as "no response" (the §1.1 sin at event granularity). The per-app buffer is bounded
  (proposal: 500 events/app, evict-oldest) and eviction is noted in the view.

**Deleted:** `internal/tui/interceptmodel.go`, `interceptview.go`, `interceptrun.go` + tests;
`runInterceptTUI`. The rendering (dividers, honest reasons, truncation notices) **moves** to the
inspector — the work is kept, only its duplicate host goes.

**Decoupling invariant holds:** `tui` imports only `model` + `mark`; the socket/log read stays in `main`
and feeds the view over a channel, as `Sampler`/`Inspector` do.

### 3.3 Daemon nudges, and the ownership model [D1]

- A foreground `intercept` run suggests `--install-daemon`, framed by what it buys: login/startup
  phone-home is not missed because it is undecryptable — it already happened before you could arm.
- `install.sh` offers the daemon at install time.
- Wording: `armed before your session starts`. Never "first" (§5.2).
- **Ownership [D1].** The daemon runs and writes as root. At install, the installing user
  (`SUDO_UID`/`SUDO_GID`) is recorded in the plist's environment; **at every startup** the daemon chowns
  the log dir, log file, and stream socket to that user (modes stay restrictive: dir 0700, file 0600,
  socket 0600 — only ownership moves). Install-time chown alone is insufficient: the socket is
  re-created root-owned at every boot. If the recorded user vanishes, reads fail with EACCES → empty
  state 2 (§3.2), honestly.
- **The daemon gains `--stream`.** Today the plist runs `intercept --yes --log=…` and, because the
  socket only defaults on when *neither* output flag is given, the encouraged deployment has no live
  stream at all. The plist passes both `--stream` and `--log`; live console follows the socket,
  catch-up reads the log (§3.2).
- **Operational logging adopts zerolog's tiers [D2].** The daemon's operational log (today ad-hoc
  prints captured by launchd as `daemon.log`/`daemon.err`) goes through zerolog with real levels,
  console mode for humans. Flow events stay level-free — they are records, not log tiers.
- Single-console-user assumption, stated plainly: one recorded console user per machine. Multi-user
  access is out of scope (group models are a policy decision, deferred — justified: the threat model is
  a single-operator workstation; widening readers of decrypted traffic needs a decision, not a default).

## 4. What this does NOT change

Read-only mirror (redact/block is Phase 3) — with the single shutdown-timing exception of §3.1.8;
cooperative coverage (evasive software still needs the NetworkExtension proxy); consent, teardown, and
the arm⇒disarm invariant; the closed honest status set.

## 5. Known behaviours recorded honestly

**5.1 We silently downgrade HTTP/2 → HTTP/1.1.** No `NextProtos` is set on either TLS config, so no ALPN
is offered and clients fall back to HTTP/1.1. Verified: live captures show `HTTP/1.1` from WhatsApp and
Proton, apps that otherwise negotiate h2. Consequences: it is why per-message framing is feasible at all;
it contradicts "byte-for-byte **unmodified**" in spirit (the payload is unmodified, the negotiation is
not) and the package doc must say so; **and a client that requires h2 may hard-fail rather than downgrade
— gRPC-over-TLS is the likely case.** Per **[D4]**, §9 step 0 measures this *before* sign-off of the
build plan. Decision rule, pre-agreed: if hard-fail is confirmed in real clients, Phase 2.5 records the
limitation honestly (documentation + an `error`-with-`Reason` event where detectable) and does **not**
take h2 support into scope — h2 framing is a different proxy, sized for a later phase; silently breaking
those clients is what we have *today* (Phase 2 shipped this), so the spike informs scope, it does not
block shipping what Phase 2 already does.

**5.2 launchd has no ordering primitive.** `man launchd.plist`: jobs *"are evaluated completely
independently by the system."* No `Before`/`After`. A system LaunchDaemon does start before any user logs
in — covering user-session apps — but concurrent system daemons are a race we cannot win.

**5.3 `br` is NOT decoded — decided: marker, not decoder [D3].** `decodeBody` handles `gzip` and
`deflate` only. The first draft of this spec claimed `br` — wrong, and the evidence was already on
screen: a live capture showed `Content-Encoding: br` *without* the `(decoded)` marker, directly beside
`gzip (decoded)`. Under per-message streaming this gap surfaces on **every** brotli exchange rather than
once per connection. Per **[D3]**: any body with an undecoded `Content-Encoding` is labelled
`(content-encoding: br — not decoded)` and its bytes are never presented as content; adding a brotli
decoder is deferred until live captures show the share justifies the dependency.

**5.4 Two truncation layers existed and only one was surfaced.** Recorded so no future reader
re-introduces a bounded buffer without a matching honest notice.

## 6. Resolved (maintainer, 2026-07-17; revised by round 2)

**6.1 `console --intercept` is DELETED — and `ReadLog` is not.** Not kept as a log reader — removed
entirely: `--intercept`, `runInterceptView`, `formatFlow`, the stdout tail, the socket/log dispatch,
their tests, **and the shipped pointers to the command** (the arming print and daemon-install print both
say `view: counterspy console --intercept=…`; the usage line follows) — replaced by the merged path
(`counterspy console` → Exfiltration). Round 2 correction: `publish.ReadLog` **survives** — §3.2's log
fallback is its caller (deleting it was right only while it had none); the write→read-through-sanitise
round-trip test (`TestLogSink_ReadBack`, including the untrusted-`Status` coercion) is rebuilt against
the internal scan path, because the log is 0600 on disk holding decrypted traffic and its write path
must stay proven.

**6.2 Never-completing responses: BOTH — terminate if we can, else opaque.** Bound and publish per the
§3.1.5 state machine (snapshot once, terminal `partial`), continue via the skip path; where framing is
impossible (§3.1.3), mark `opaque` and relay on.

**6.3 Opaque publishes ONCE per connection. Never repeated.** No periodic "still opaque", no byte-count
refreshes, no close event restating it. A long-lived opaque connection must not become a heartbeat — and
round 2 extended the same rule to *decrypted* connections: the §3.1.5 state machine guarantees at most
two events per message, so no message type can become a heartbeat either. §6a.1's transition
(`decrypted` messages, then one `opaque` at upgrade) is a status *change*, not a repeat — approved
with `Reason` set (**[D5]**).

**6.4 Attribution is per CONNECTION, reused by every message on it.** `portOwner` already runs once per
connection; per-message lookups would fork lsof at message rate.

**6a.3 → closed here.** `sanitizeMessage` caps `App` (§3.2), closing the untrusted-producer name bound
at the ingestion boundary rather than at draw.

## 7. Testing strategy

- **Framing:** a keep-alive connection with N pipelined exchanges publishes 2N direction-events in
  completion order. **`HEAD` must not desync** (the canonical case: `Content-Length` present, no body).
  `204`/`304` likewise. `100-continue`/`103` must not consume a queue entry. **Early final response
  (mandatory):** server rejects an `Expect: 100-continue` upload with 413 and the client aborts the body
  — the response still publishes (§3.1.1's push-at-head), the request publishes terminal `partial`, no
  wedge. **Trailers:** an `aws-chunked`-style message with trailer fields does not desync the next
  message (§3.1.2).
- **Desync ⇒ opaque:** deliberately corrupt framing mid-connection → exactly one `opaque` **with
  `Reason`**, no fabricated messages, relay continues. Empty-pop and queue-overflow each flip opaque once.
- **Bounds:** a >1 MB message publishes terminal `partial` **and the connection stays framed** (the next
  message parses correctly — this is what proves 3.1.2's skip path). A never-completing response
  publishes exactly one `streaming` snapshot — then **no further events** until termination (the
  no-heartbeat test §6.3 demands). A header block over `MaxHeaderBytes` flips `opaque` without a memory
  spike. The per-app buffer evicts at its bound and says so.
- **Close/abort:** client `SHUT_WR` after its request — the response still completes and publishes;
  connection close mid-body publishes terminal `partial` + `Reason: "connection closed"`; the idle reap
  follows the same path.
- **Masking under truncation (mandatory):** a secret cut mid-token at the `partial` boundary is still
  masked — the §1.4 regression, now a permanent test. Also the chunk-spanning secret this design buys.
- **Schema:** an old-format record produces exactly one `error` event with the version `Reason`, a
  rejection count, and an uninterrupted feed — never an empty `decrypted` message, never an abort.
- **Merge:** with a live stream, inspect on a *proxied* row (loopback endpoint) renders that app's
  decrypted messages with real destinations; counterspy's own process is absent from the rollup (and
  from `Concern()` inputs); without a daemon, the bpf verdict and empty state 1; EACCES produces empty
  state 2, never state 1. The inspector's buffer is fed only via the sanitise-gated path. Decoupling
  invariant still passes.
- **Regression:** `pinned`/`opaque`/`error` still publish exactly once per connection, never silently,
  always with `Reason`. The log write→read-through-sanitise round trip (§6.1) is rebuilt.
- **Spike (§9 step 0) is a gate, not a test:** its result lands in §5.1 before the build plan is signed.
- Gate: `-race` ×2, both builds, `architext validate`, coverage ≥ 80%.

## 8. Risks

- **Masking regression is the top risk**, and §1.4 proved it is not theoretical: bounded capture already
  defeated the body-secret rule once, in shipped code. Every partial-publish path must mask what it has,
  and the truncation-boundary test is mandatory, not optional.
- **Framing complexity is the second.** The correlation queue + custom skip reader + trailer handling +
  half-close semantics is real HTTP-proxy machinery replacing a byte sniffer. The mitigation is that
  every failure has one honest destination (`opaque`, once, with `Reason`) rather than a resync
  heuristic.
- **Wire-format churn:** socket, rotating log, reader and `sanitizeMessage` all change at once; old logs
  get exactly one loud version error (§3.1.7) — neither silence nor a crash-loop of notices.
- **Scope creep into Exfiltration:** the merge touches a large working subsystem, and round 2 *added*
  scope to it (proxy-endpoint recognition, self-exclusion, log fallback, drop/eviction honesty). That
  scope is the price of the merge telling the truth; it does not extend to redesigning the view itself.
- **This reworks shipped, verified code.** v0.7.0 is cuttable today. Phase 2.5 lands on its own branch
  off `develop` **after** that cut, not by holding the release open.
