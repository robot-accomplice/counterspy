# Phase 2.5 — Streaming messages, and merging Intercept into Exfiltration

**Status:** REVISION 4 (2026-07-17). Three adversarial rounds are resolved here. Round 1 killed
independent-direction parsing and found the live secret leak (`419d421`). Round 2 (`cp-spec2.5-r2`, 14
findings) killed the merge join-key, the queue push-point, daemon readability, and the unconstrained
state machine. Round 3 (`cp-spec2.5-r3`, 20 findings) attacked revision 3's *fixes* — and beat up the
ALPN-bypass proposal (C4) at the maintainer's request: verdict ADOPT-WITH-CHANGES, folded into §5.1 as
the formal amendment of [D4]. Maintainer decisions marked **[D1]–[D5]**, challenge resolutions **C1–C4**.
**Predecessor:** `2026-07-15-phase2-tls-interception-design.md` (shipped; verified live 2026-07-16).

## 0. What revision 4 changed

Round 3 found that several round-2 *fixes* re-created the bug they closed. The repairs, per section:

- **Ownership (§3.3, C1 — replaces [D1]'s chown model).** "Daemon chowns its artifacts to the console
  user" was itself a designed-in privesc (user-owned dir + root path-based chown/open follows symlinks)
  and died on rotation (new files born root 0600). Replaced with the maintainer-approved **group model**:
  `root:admin`, correct ownership **at birth** (setgid dir + explicit mode + `O_NOFOLLOW`), no chown
  ever, and the confidentiality trade stated in writing.
- **The join is re-pinned on provenance (§3.2).** Round 2's "join-key doesn't exist" fix moved the bug:
  intercept names apps from lsof's 16-char `p_comm`, egress from the full executable path — the keys
  differ for any long-named process. The join is now keyed by **executable path** both sides
  (`proc_pidpath` per connection — also closes ticket T-4's want), with PID+name as display, not key.
- **The state machine is actually closed now (§3.1.5).** Round 3 produced three distinct orderings the
  revision-3 text allowed and the viewer couldn't survive: double-terminal (cap + later completion),
  streaming-*after*-terminal (uncancelled age timer), and orphaned-`streaming`-forever (desync with no
  terminal). All three are ruled out by construction.
- **Log fallback survives contact with rotation and the socket seam (§3.2).** Connect-then-read ordering,
  rotated-file inclusion, and dedup-by-key. Plus ConnID carries a process epoch so a 7-day log can't
  collide two daemons' connections.
- **C4 (ALPN-aware bypass) adopted with the beater's changes (§5.1).** It amends [D4] formally; the
  spike becomes a *validation* matrix, not a go/no-go.

## 1. Why

Phase 2 works — it decrypts real traffic, attributes it to apps, and reverts cleanly. Live use and
review exposed four faults.

**1.1 The capture is truncated and the UI implied it wasn't.** The proxy keeps `FlowCaptureBytes` (8 KB)
per direction in a `capBuf` and publishes one record at connection close. A real session captured a
26,836-byte Sentry POST from Proton Mail — ~70% never recorded. The view then capped the *display* at 8
lines and printed `… (5 more lines)`, implying content that did not exist. The display cap is fixed
(`9952a6c`); the **capture** cap remains, and buffer-then-truncate is the wrong shape.

**1.2 Flows are stamped at open, published at close.** Keep-alive connections surface after shorter ones
that started later (`14:44:58` printed above `14:44:28`). The viewer sorts by `At` to compensate — a
workaround for a record shape that does not match reality. (`At` is *seconds* precision, so even the
workaround has no total order across a browser's sub-second burst — see §3.1.6.)

**1.3 Intercepted and Exfiltration are the same thing.** Exfiltration asks *"this app is trusted; what is
it sending?"* and its inspector answers with content until it hits `ENCRYPTED (TLS) — metadata only`.
Intercept removes that wall; it does not ask a new question. The split produced a duplicate per-app
rollup beside a per-app rollup, and the Intercepted view was rebuilt four times because the split was
accepted rather than questioned.

**1.4 Bounded capture defeated masking (FIXED, `419d421`).** `reBodySecret`'s value alternation required
a closing quote, so a body cut mid-value (`"api_key":"sk_live_ABC`) matched neither branch and published
the partial secret **in the clear**. Reproduced against the live pipeline. Fixed by adding the dangling
open-quote case, mirroring `rePEMOpen`. **This is a precondition for §6.2**: any design that publishes
partial text must assume truncation lands mid-token, and the masking rules must survive it.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Per-message streaming

The relay stays a **byte-for-byte mirror**: bytes are forwarded verbatim, and a *copy* is parsed. We do
NOT become a re-serialising HTTP proxy — that would change what the server sees and break the read-only
invariant. (One shutdown-timing exception, honestly recorded: §3.1.8.)

**3.1.1 Framing requires request↔response correlation.** The two parse goroutines share a **request
queue** with exact semantics, pinned because round 2 proved "as each request completes" is fatal:

- The request parser pushes `{method, seq}` when the request **head** completes (request line + headers),
  *not* when the body finishes. The head carries the only framing fact the response parser needs (the
  method); body completion must not gate response framing.
- The response parser pops one entry per **final** response (`HEAD` → no body regardless of
  `Content-Length`; `204`/`304` → no body).
- **Interim 1xx (`100`, `103`, …):** frame, publish nothing, do **not** pop. **`101` is not covered by
  this rule** — 101 is the upgrade trigger of §3.1.3/§6a.1, carved out explicitly so no implementer
  coin-flip exists.
- **Empty-pop is a desync.** A response head arriving with an empty queue means framing is lost →
  §3.1.3, `opaque` once.
- **The queue is bounded** (`MaxPipelinedRequests`, proposal 64 — browsers pipeline ≤6). Overflow →
  §3.1.3, `opaque` once. Entries live until popped or connection end (64 × ~40 bytes — the bound is
  what matters, not the reclaim).
- An RFC 7230 **early final response** (server rejects `Expect: 100-continue`, early 401/413) frames
  correctly: the entry was pushed at head completion, the response publishes normally, and the aborted
  request body is §3.1.8's close/abort case, not a desync.

**3.1.2 The pump needs its OWN framing reader.** `inspect.readBody` must **not** be reused: it is a
one-shot *preview* reader (`io.ReadAll(io.LimitReader(body, maxRawBody))` then `Close()`) that never
drains the remainder — reusing it guarantees permanent desync. The pump's reader separates *how much to
keep* (bounded, for display) from *how much to skip* (all of it, to stay framed):

- `Content-Length`: keep min(N, cap), skip the rest by count.
- `chunked`: walk chunk headers, keep bounded payload, skip the rest — **and consume the trailer-part**:
  after the terminal `0` chunk, read trailer fields until the blank line (RFC 7230 §4.1.2), whether or
  not `Trailer` was declared. S3 `aws-chunked` checksums and gRPC-web trailers are legitimate and common
  in exactly the traffic we watch; a walker that stops at `0\r\n\r\n` mis-frames the *next* message.

This is what makes §6.2's "publish a partial and keep framing" achievable at all.

**3.1.3 Desync ⇒ opaque, once.** Any framing failure — an unparseable message, a protocol upgrade
(`101`), an empty-pop or queue overflow (§3.1.1), header block over the cap (§3.1.5), anything that stops
being HTTP/1.1 — resolves to §6.2's second branch: mark the connection `opaque` **once** (§6.3), with
`Reason` set (§3.1.6), stop parsing, keep relaying. There is no "resync after garbage" path; attempting
one would publish fabricated messages, which is worse than admitting we lost framing. The C4 bypass
(§5.1) lands here too: `opaque` at decision time, pump never attaches.

**3.1.4 Events are PER DIRECTION, not paired.** A paired request+response event must wait for the
response, which (a) delays showing a request whose response is slow or never arrives, and (b)
re-introduces §1.2's ordering bug at message granularity when a response completes before a
slow-uploading request. Instead each direction publishes when **it** completes, carrying `ConnID`+`Seq`
so the viewer groups them for display. A request that never gets a response still appears — honestly, as
a request with no response. (Approved: **[D5]**.)

**3.1.5 Bounds, and the per-message event state machine — closed, this time.** Per-message is not
unbounded, and rounds 2–3 showed the *states* must be pinned by construction, not aspiration:

- `MaxMessageBytes` (proposal: 1 MB) and `MaxMessageAge` (proposal: 30 s) bound one message.
- **One message produces at most two events, ever, in this order:**
  1. At most one **`streaming`** snapshot — fired if `MaxMessageAge` elapses before the terminal event.
     Never repeats: a never-ending SSE/long-poll/LLM stream yields one snapshot and then silence. This
     is §6.3's no-heartbeat rule applied to *decrypted* connections.
  2. Exactly one **terminal** event: **`complete`** (fully captured) or **`partial`** — "this is all you
     will ever get" — published the moment the bound is hit, or at normal completion, or at
     close/error mid-message (§3.1.8). `partial` always carries `Reason`.
- **The trigger overlap is resolved:** hitting `MaxMessageBytes` publishes the terminal `partial`
  immediately; the skip path then drains the body *without further events* — a body that was capped and
  later completes does **not** get a second terminal. The age timer is **cancelled at the terminal
  event**; a `streaming` snapshot can never follow a terminal. Order is `streaming?` → terminal, and
  nothing after terminal, by construction.
- **No orphaned `streaming`:** HTTP/1.1 is serial per direction, so a connection has at most one
  in-flight message per direction. On desync (§3.1.3) or close with an open `streaming` entry, the pump
  publishes a terminal `partial` for it (`Reason: "framing lost"` / `"connection closed"`) alongside the
  connection-level event — at most two such closures per connection, so §6.3's once-rule is untouched
  and the viewer's "terminal event follows" promise always holds.
- The viewer *replaces* a `streaming` entry with its terminal successor (same `ConnID`+`Seq`+`Direction`)
  — never appends a duplicate.
- **The header block is bounded by us, full stop.** Round 2 struck the claim that `net/textproto`
  applies its own ceiling — it does not (`ReadMIMEHeader` is unlimited; `DefaultMaxHeaderBytes` exists
  only inside `http.Server`). The pump imposes `MaxHeaderBytes` (proposal: 64 KB) per header block;
  over-cap → §3.1.3. The same bound is applied to the proxy's existing CONNECT read
  (`proxy.go:70-72`), which today accepts unbounded headers from any local client (ticket T-20) —
  folded into this phase because it is the same mechanism in the same file.
- **Decoded size is already bounded** and must stay so: `decodeBody`'s `io.LimitReader(r, maxBodyPreview+1)`
  caps *decoded* output regardless of input, so compression bombs are handled today. Do not regress this.

**3.1.6 Model shape.**

```
InterceptedMessage {
  SchemaVersion int     // REQUIRED — see 3.1.7
  ConnID   string  // <proxy-start-epoch>-<seq> — unique across daemon restarts, so a 7-day log
                   // can never collide two processes' connection 7s (round 3)
  Seq      int     // exchange sequence within the connection; 0 for connection-level events
  Direction string  // request | response; EMPTY for connection-level events
  At       string  // RFC3339Nano, when THIS event completed — nano precision + (ConnID,Seq) tiebreak
                   // gives the viewer a total order even in the merged log+socket corpus
  PID      int
  App      string  // display name
  Path     string  // resolved executable path (proc_pidpath) — THE JOIN KEY for §3.2; "" if unresolved
  DestName, DestIP, SNI string
  Status   string  // decrypted | pinned | opaque | error (closed set, unchanged)
  Text     string  // decoded + masked; "" for non-decrypted statuses
  Bytes    int     // wire bytes observed for this message AT PUBLISH TIME; unused on Seq-0 events
  Total    int     // -1 = unknown (chunked/undeclared); 0 = declared zero-length; >0 = Content-Length
  State    string  // message events only: complete | partial | streaming. Connection-level events
                   // (Seq 0) carry State "" — and sanitize passes "" through on Seq-0, per event class
  Reason   string  // why: partial cause / opaque cause / error / pinned detail; "" when unremarkable
}
```

- Message events (Seq > 0) carry `Status: "decrypted"`; the `State` field carries completeness. Seq-0
  connection-level events carry the non-decrypted `Status` (`pinned`/`opaque`/`error`) and empty
  `State`.
- `ConnID` epoch is the proxy process start time in Unix nanoseconds, suffixed with a short random
  value so collisions are impossible even if the clock repeats.
- If a `streaming` snapshot is dropped and only its terminal event reaches the viewer, the viewer
  renders the terminal event normally; the missing snapshot must not be interpreted as "no response".

- `State` distinguishes two facts a reader must not conflate: **`partial`** = all you will ever get;
  **`streaming`** = a bounded snapshot whose terminal event follows — and §3.1.5 guarantees it does.
- `pinned` / `opaque` / `error` remain **per-connection** facts published exactly once (§6.3), with
  `Seq: 0`, empty `Direction`/`Text`/`State`, and `Reason` **always set** — today the reason is recorded
  (`DestName: "(unresolved: …)"`); dropping it would make a desync epidemic un-root-causable (Rule 14).
- `Bytes` is honestly *observed-so-far*: for a capped/closed `partial` the true total may be unknowable.
  `Total` carries the peer's declared expectation when one exists; no correction events are published.

**3.1.7 The wire format must be versioned — and "loud" has an address.** `json.Unmarshal` silently
ignores unknown fields and leaves missing ones zero — so an OLD log record read by NEW code parses
without error into an *empty but apparently complete* `decrypted` message. That is the §1.1 sin served
by the reader instead of the writer. `SchemaVersion` is required, and rejection is loud **through the
event stream, not around it**:

- The one ingestion function (§3.2) checks `SchemaVersion` *before* best-effort parsing. A record with a
  missing or newer version is **not parsed**. On the first mismatch per stream, ingestion synthesises
  exactly one `error` event — carrying the *current* `SchemaVersion` (so it passes the sanitize gate it
  must travel through) and `Reason: "unsupported record version (got X, want 2) — is the daemon the
  same build?"` — injected **after** sanitisation, and further mismatches are only counted.
- The synthesized event has no app, so it does **not** go to the per-app buffer: it lands in a
  stream-level notices channel shown in the inspector chrome **with its `Reason`** — a bare
  `3 records rejected` counter without the message is not loud, it's a shrug. Spoofing is bounded by
  design (one notice per stream; a socket writer can already forge entire flows, so the notice adds no
  new capability) and is recorded, not hand-waved.
- Malformed-JSON lines: counted and summarised the same way, never one-notice-per-line. Per-record
  loudness through an untrusted socket is a spam weapon; per-stream notice + counter is loud without
  being weaponisable. The feed is never aborted over a bad record.
- Socket and log carry the **same** record schema (**[D2]→C2**: documented plain JSON, not logger
  framing); both readers share this path.

**3.1.8 Close, half-close, and abort are first-class — with the TLS mechanics named.** Rounds 2–3 found
the terminations unspecified. Rules:

- The relay's current behaviour — closing **both** conns on the **first** direction's EOF
  (`proxy.go:265-268`) — changes: each direction ends independently. A client `SHUT_WR` after its
  request must not truncate an in-flight response. This is a behavioural change to the mirror (still
  byte-verbatim forwarding; only shutdown timing) and is recorded here as the one exception.
- **No FIN propagation:** `crypto/tls` has no `CloseWrite` — closing write under a live `tls.Conn` would
  send `close_notify` and TCP FIN, a protocol-level truncation the upstream may fatal on. A half-close
  is therefore *not* forwarded; instead, when a direction ends (EOF/error), the surviving direction gets
  a **linger deadline** (`MaxLinger`, proposal 30 s ≈ `MaxMessageAge`) to finish its in-flight message,
  then the connection closes. This bounds the abandoned-connection cost at churn × 30 s of 2 fds + 2
  goroutines (the 5-minute `idleTimeout` remains as backstop) and bounds how long an orphaned
  `streaming` entry can live. A write error ends only the writing direction — which is also how a dead
  client is detected when bytes flow. This rule assumes HTTP/1.1 bodies are framed by `Content-Length`
  or chunked `Transfer-Encoding`; peers that rely on TCP half-close to signal end-of-body may stall.
- A connection that ends mid-message publishes the in-flight message as terminal **`partial`** with
  `Reason: "connection closed"` (or the error). No abort state is added — `partial` already means "all
  you will ever get" — but close **is** an explicit `partial` trigger.

### 3.2 Merge into Exfiltration

**The merge point is the inspector's verdict, not a tab.** With a live daemon, `ENCRYPTED (TLS) —
metadata only` gains an answer. Three rounds proved the join is where this design lives or dies; it is
pinned below by *provenance*, not field names.

- **The join key is the executable path.** The egress rollup already keys groups by binary path
  (`Aggregate`). Intercept attribution must therefore carry the same key: `portOwner` resolves the
  connection-owning PID's **executable path** (`proc_pidpath`, one syscall per connection — and closes
  the long-standing T-4 want), and `InterceptedMessage.Path` joins to the egress row's `Path`. Display
  names (`App`) are labels only: lsof's `p_comm` truncates at 16 chars, so name-keyed joins miss
  exactly on long-named helpers. PID alone is not a key (reuse across a 7-day log); PID+name is the
  fallback only when path resolution fails, and that fallback is counted and shown.
- **Proxy-endpoint recognition.** `main` knows the armed proxy endpoint (default
  `127.0.0.1:interceptProxyPort`) and passes it into the view **as data** — no `internal` package
  imports `package main`, and the decoupling invariant is untouched. An egress connection leaf to that
  endpoint is a **proxied** connection: its displayed destination comes from the message buffer, never
  from nettop. When no daemon is armed, such a leaf is labelled `local proxy endpoint (not intercepted)`
  — no join is attempted and no false "proxied" claim is made (any process can connect to or squat a
  free port).
- **What the inspector renders.** Inspect (`i`) on a proxied app's row renders that app's intercept
  messages: decrypted, masked, time-ordered by the §3.1.6 total order, with dividers, honest
  non-decrypted reasons and truncation notices already built. Destination scoping comes from the
  *messages* (`DestName`/`DestIP`), because every proxied connection of an app shares the one loopback
  endpoint.
- **Self-exclusion, precisely scoped.** Only the proxy's **relay dials** are excluded from the rollup:
  connections owned by the counterspy process whose endpoint appears as a `DestIP` in the intercept
  stream. Those bytes are already shown under their real apps; counting them again double-counts every
  proxied byte and feeds `Concern()` until the tool flags itself. counterspy's **own** egress (feedback
  POSTs, anything not matching an intercepted destination) **stays visible** — the tool does not exempt
  itself from the surveillance it performs, and no binary at our path gets a blanket pass (only
  connections already attributed to real apps via the stream are excluded). The footnote says exactly
  this: `counterspy relay connections are shown under the apps that own them; counterspy's own traffic stays.`
  Until the first intercept message naming a destination arrives, an egress conn to that destination
  owned by counterspy may briefly appear in the rollup; the view removes it retroactively once the
  stream proves it is a relay target.
- **Row renderer changes — enumerated, not "unchanged."** The rollup data path is unchanged (nettop/lsof
  per app); three renderer behaviors are new: proxied-leaf destination labels from the buffer, the
  not-armed proxy-endpoint label, and the exclusion footnote. Everything else about the view is
  untouched (§8's no-redesign rule stands).
- **Log fallback [C1][C2].** Entering Exfiltration with a daemon installed: **connect the live socket
  first** (buffering events as they arrive), **then read the log** — rotated files oldest→newest,
  bounded — and merge by `(ConnID, Seq, Direction)` with replace semantics (terminal replaces
  `streaming`; identical events collapse). Connect-first closes the loss window between log-EOF and
  socket-connect; the ConnID epoch (§3.1.6) makes dedup exact across daemon restarts. This is the
  daemon's entire justification — captures made while no console ran — so the rotated history
  (`.1`…`.keep`) is in scope, not just the active file.
- **Honest empty states — three, never one lie:**
  1. No daemon installed: `No live intercept daemon found. Showing only unencrypted traffic.`
  2. Daemon installed but artifacts unreadable (EACCES): `intercept daemon is installed but its log is
     not readable by this user — repair: re-run sudo counterspy intercept --install-daemon`
     (re-install is idempotent and re-applies §3.3's ownership; **no new CLI surface** — the flag set is
     frozen and `--repair-daemon` is rejected by it).
  3. Daemon installed and readable, nothing captured yet: `daemon running — no intercepted messages yet.`
- **Sudo prominence:** make the existing startup warning prominent; re-state on entering Exfiltration:
  `No traffic found — is the intercept daemon running as sudo?`
- **Single ingestion point (required).** The per-app message buffer the inspector queries MUST be fed
  *only* through the sanitise-gated read path (`ReadSocket`/`ReadLog` → `scanMessages` →
  `sanitizeMessage`). The socket is untrusted input (anyone who can write it). `sanitizeMessage`
  (rewritten from `sanitizeFlow`) caps `App`/`Path` (closing §6a.3), caps text fields, coerces unknown
  `Status`/`State` *per event class* (message events vs Seq-0 connection events, which legitimately
  carry `State: ""`), and performs §3.1.7's version gate. The plan names this one function.
- **Drop and eviction honesty.** The socket→view channel is bounded; a slow console drops events and the
  drop counter is rendered (`N events dropped — console too slow`) — a dropped response event must never
  masquerade as "no response" (the §1.1 sin at event granularity). The per-app buffer is bounded
  (proposal: 500 events/app, evict-oldest) and eviction is noted in the view.

**Deleted:** `internal/tui/interceptmodel.go`, `interceptview.go`, `interceptrun.go` + tests;
`runInterceptTUI`. The rendering (dividers, honest reasons, truncation notices) **moves** to the
inspector — the work is kept, only its duplicate host goes.

**Decoupling invariant holds:** `tui` imports only `model` + `mark`; the socket/log read stays in `main`
and feeds the view over a channel, as `Sampler`/`Inspector` do; the armed proxy endpoint travels the
same channel as data.

### 3.3 Daemon nudges, and the ownership model [D1]→C1

- A foreground `intercept` run suggests `--install-daemon`, framed by what it buys: login/startup
  phone-home is not missed because it is undecryptable — it already happened before you could arm.
- `install.sh` offers the daemon at install time.
- Wording: `armed before your session starts`. Never "first" (§5.2).
- **Ownership is a birth property, not a chown (C1).** Round 3 killed the chown model: a user-owned dir
  with a root writer is a symlink-away from a user→root write primitive, chown-at-startup races every
  restart, rotation re-creates files root-owned, and UID recycling silently hands old logs to new users.
  The group model removes the whole class:
  - Log dir `/var/log/counterspy` and socket dir: `root:admin`, **0750 + setgid** (files inherit the
    group at creation — no chown, ever).
  - Log files: `root:admin` **0640 from birth** (explicit mode at create; rotation preserves it; the
    rotated `.1`…`.keep` files are born the same way, so the §3.2 catch-up can read history).
  - Stream socket: `root:admin` **0660** (macOS enforces write permission on unix-socket connect;
    group-write is exactly the console's need), with an explicit chmod after bind — umask-dependent
    modes are not a security boundary.
  - Opens use `O_NOFOLLOW` and a regular-file check (defense in depth; the codebase already has the
    pattern in `removeIfSocket`).
  - **The confidentiality trade, in writing:** any non-root-readable design hands every process running
    as an admin user the decrypted capture of every app on the machine, including bodies masking does
    not cover (T-18's free-form secrets). The maintainer accepted this trade (C1) over the rejected
    alternative (console-always-sudo). On a single-admin machine, admin ≈ the console user; non-admin
    users get nothing. It is stated here so a future reader cannot mistake it for an oversight.
- **The daemon gains `--stream`.** Today the plist runs `intercept --yes --log=…` and, because the
  socket only defaults on when *neither* output flag is given, the encouraged deployment has no live
  stream at all. The plist passes both `--stream` and `--log`; live console follows the socket,
  catch-up reads the log (§3.2). The plan asserts the rendered plist (round 3: the fatal-flaw-C fix had
  no test).
- **Operational logging adopts zerolog's tiers [D2]→C2.** The daemon's operational log (today ad-hoc
  prints captured by launchd as `daemon.log`/`daemon.err`) goes through zerolog with real levels,
  console mode for humans. Flow events stay level-free — they are records, not log tiers.
- Single-console-user assumption, stated plainly: one admin console population per machine. Multi-user
  access models are out of scope (deferred — justified: the threat model is a single-operator
  workstation; widening readers of decrypted traffic is a policy decision, not a default).

## 4. What this does NOT change

Read-only mirror (redact/block is Phase 3) — with the single shutdown-timing exception of §3.1.8;
cooperative coverage (evasive software still needs the NetworkExtension proxy); consent, teardown, and
the arm⇒disarm invariant; the closed honest status set.

## 5. Known behaviours recorded honestly

**5.1 We silently downgrade HTTP/2 → HTTP/1.1 — and h2-only clients are bypassed (C4, amends [D4]).**
No `NextProtos` is set on either TLS config, so no ALPN is offered and clients fall back to HTTP/1.1.
Verified: live captures show `HTTP/1.1` from WhatsApp and Proton, apps that otherwise negotiate h2.
Consequences: it is why per-message framing is feasible at all; it contradicts "byte-for-byte
**unmodified**" in spirit (the payload is unmodified, the negotiation is not) and the package doc must
say so; and a client that requires h2 hard-fails rather than downgrades — **verified, not assumed:**
grpc-go ≥ 1.67 enforces client-side ALPN (`missing selected ALPN property`), and today that failure is
*stamped* `decrypted` **with zero bytes** in our own record — a silent dishonest row in shipped code.

**The bypass (C4, beaten up and adopted with changes).** Before terminating, the proxy peeks at the raw
ClientHello; if ALPN offers `h2` and not `http/1.1`, it does **not** MITM — it dials the target **plain
TCP** (never the TLS `defaultDial`) and blind-relays, publishing `opaque` at decision time with
`Reason: "h2-only client — bypassed"` (§3.1.3's destination: pump never attaches). Rules the beat-up
added:

- **Fail-open, always.** Any peek ambiguity — partial record, fragmented handshake message, ECH-hidden
  ALPN, garbage — falls through to today's MITM path. The peek may never break a connection that works
  today. ECH hiding ALPN ⇒ normal path, no regression, and this is written down so it isn't
  "discovered" later.
- **Exact trigger:** ALPN non-empty ∧ contains `h2` ∧ lacks `http/1.1`. Browsers (`[h2, http/1.1]`) are
  still MITM'd and downgraded (disclosed above). Draft tokens and prior-knowledge h2 are non-issues,
  ruled so, not left to chance.
- **The peek is the only decision point.** Detecting via `GetCertificate`/`ClientHelloInfo.SupportedProtos`
  is refuted: aborting there makes `crypto/tls` write an alert to the client — the very breakage being
  avoided — and the raw ClientHello is unrecoverable for replay (PSK binders, extension order).
  `NextProtos: ["http/1.1"]` is strictly worse: `no_application_protocol` hard-fails even
  non-enforcing h2 clients.
- **One parser.** ALPN extraction *extends* `internal/inspect/tls.go` (today it walks only SNI) — one
  parser, one test corpus, same fail-open tolerance. The peek threads `readConnect`'s buffered reader
  (a pipelined CONNECT+ClientHello is otherwise mis-peeked; that over-read is a latent Phase-2 bug,
  ticket T-21, fixed incidentally here). Mechanically, `readConnect` keeps the `bufio.Reader` it hands
  to `http.ReadRequest`; after the CONNECT request is consumed, any bytes already buffered beyond it
  belong to the ClientHello and are extracted. A wrapping `net.Conn` replays those buffered bytes on
  the first `Read` and then delegates to the underlying connection. That wrapper is handed to
  `tls.Server` on the MITM path or to the plain-TCP bypass relay, so neither path loses the pipelined
  handshake bytes. The peek shares the single existing `handshakeTimeout` deadline — no double-arming.
  A ClientHello fragmented across TLS records is a documented non-trigger (fail-open).
- **The honest cost:** gRPC-class traffic becomes *working but invisible* — an evasion avenue inside
  the cooperative model, published as an attributed `opaque` with `Reason`, which is strictly better
  than today's dishonest empty `decrypted` row or a hard failure the user blames on counterspy. It is
  also *less* code in the trust boundary for those connections (no leaf minted, nothing decrypted).
- **The spike [D4] now validates, not decides** (§9 step 0): a real-client matrix — grpc-go ≥1.67 and
  <1.67, grpc-swift/Cronet, an h2 browser — confirming the bypass fires exactly on h2-only and that
  bypassed connections complete.

**5.2 launchd has no ordering primitive.** `man launchd.plist`: jobs *"are evaluated completely
independently by the system."* No `Before`/`After`. A system LaunchDaemon does start before any user logs
in — covering user-session apps — but concurrent system daemons are a race we cannot win.

**5.3 `br` IS decoded (C3).** Round 2's either/or (marker vs decoder) resolved to decoder:
`andybalholm/brotli` behind the existing `decodeBody` switch, in-process and small, per the maintainer's
dependency rule (in-process deps welcome with footprint judgment). The first draft of this spec claimed
`br` support that never existed — wrong, and the evidence was already on screen; with C3 the gap closes
rather than being labelled. `zstd` remains undecoded and is labelled `(content-encoding: zstd — not
decoded)` — the marker pattern survives for everything we don't decode.

**5.4 Two truncation layers existed and only one was surfaced.** Recorded so no future reader
re-introduces a bounded buffer without a matching honest notice.

## 6. Resolved (maintainer, 2026-07-17; revised by rounds 2–3)

**6.1 `console --intercept` is DELETED — and `ReadLog` is not.** Not kept as a log reader — removed
entirely: `--intercept`, `runInterceptView`, `formatFlow`, the stdout tail, the socket/log dispatch,
their tests, **and the shipped pointers to the command** (the arming print and daemon-install print both
say `view: counterspy console --intercept=…`; the usage line follows) — replaced by the merged path
(`counterspy console` → Exfiltration). `publish.ReadLog` **survives** — §3.2's log fallback is its
caller (deleting it was right only while it had none); the write→read-through-sanitise round-trip test
(`TestLogSink_ReadBack`, including the untrusted-`Status` coercion) is rebuilt against the internal scan
path, because the log holds decrypted traffic and its write path must stay proven.

**6.2 Never-completing responses: BOTH — terminate if we can, else opaque.** Bound and publish per the
§3.1.5 state machine (snapshot once, terminal `partial`), continue via the skip path; where framing is
impossible (§3.1.3), mark `opaque` and relay on.

**6.3 Opaque publishes ONCE per connection. Never repeated.** No periodic "still opaque", no byte-count
refreshes, no close event restating it. A long-lived opaque connection must not become a heartbeat — and
the same rule covers *decrypted* connections: the §3.1.5 state machine guarantees at most two events per
message, so no message type can become a heartbeat either. §6a.1's transition (`decrypted` messages,
then one `opaque` at upgrade) is a status *change*, not a repeat — approved with `Reason` set
(**[D5]**); the pre-upgrade exchange (request + 101 response) publishes as normal `decrypted` events
first (**§3.1.1's 101 carve-out**), because it is fully framable and frequently carries auth.

**6.4 Attribution is per CONNECTION, reused by every message on it.** `portOwner` runs once per
connection; per-message lookups would fork lsof at message rate. It now also resolves the executable
path once per connection (§3.2) — same once-per-connection budget.

## 7. Testing strategy

- **Framing:** a keep-alive connection with N pipelined exchanges publishes 2N direction-events in
  completion order. **`HEAD` must not desync**. `204`/`304` likewise. `100`/`103` must not consume a
  queue entry; **`101` triggers the upgrade path, not the 1xx rule.** **Early final response
  (mandatory):** server rejects an `Expect: 100-continue` upload with 413 and the client aborts the body
  — the response still publishes, the request publishes terminal `partial`, no wedge. **Trailers:** an
  `aws-chunked`-style message with trailer fields does not desync the next message.
- **State machine (the round-3 orderings, each named):** capped-then-completed body ⇒ exactly one
  terminal (`partial`), never a second; age timer cancelled at terminal ⇒ no streaming-after-terminal;
  desync/close with an open `streaming` ⇒ terminal `partial` with `"framing lost"`/`"connection closed"`
  (≤2 per connection); viewer replaces streaming→terminal, never appends.
- **Desync ⇒ opaque:** deliberately corrupt framing mid-connection → exactly one `opaque` **with
  `Reason`**, no fabricated messages, relay continues. Empty-pop and queue-overflow each flip opaque
  once.
- **Bounds:** a >1 MB message publishes terminal `partial` **and the connection stays framed**. A
  never-completing response publishes exactly one `streaming` snapshot — then **no further events**
  until termination. A header block over `MaxHeaderBytes` flips `opaque` without a memory spike — and
  the **CONNECT read** gets the same bound (T-20, tested at the proxy, not just the pump). The per-app
  buffer evicts at its bound and says so; the socket→view **drop counter** renders when nonzero.
- **Close/abort:** client `SHUT_WR` after its request — the response completes within `MaxLinger` and
  publishes; abandoned half-closed connections are reaped at the linger deadline, not the 5-minute idle
  backstop; close mid-body ⇒ terminal `partial` + `Reason: "connection closed"`.
- **Masking under truncation (mandatory):** a secret cut mid-token at the `partial` boundary is still
  masked — the §1.4 regression, now a permanent test. Also the chunk-spanning secret this design buys.
- **Schema:** an old-format record produces exactly one `error` event (with current `SchemaVersion`, so
  it survives the gate) carrying the version `Reason` **in the chrome**, a rejection count, and an
  uninterrupted feed — never an empty `decrypted` message, never an abort, never a per-record storm.
- **Merge:** with a live stream, inspect on a *proxied* row renders that app's decrypted messages with
  real destinations; **a >16-char-named helper joins correctly by `Path`** (the QA F-5 repro); the
  not-armed proxy endpoint gets its honest label; counterspy's relay dials are absent from the rollup
  and `Concern()` inputs **while its own feedback egress stays visible**; without a daemon, the bpf
  verdict and empty state 1; EACCES produces empty state 2 (and its repair instruction uses only the
  approved flag set). The inspector's buffer is fed only via the sanitise-gated path. Decoupling
  invariant still passes.
- **Log fallback:** rotation during an unattended window — catch-up reads `.1`…`.keep` + active; an
  event published during console open arrives exactly once (connect-first + dedup), none lost in the
  seam; a daemon restart mid-log does not collide `ConnID`s (epoch) or mis-replace entries.
- **Ownership:** log dir/files/socket are `root:admin` with the §3.3 modes **at birth, including after
  rotation**; opens refuse symlinks; the rendered plist contains both `--stream` and `--log`.
- **Bypass (§5.1):** synthetic ClientHellos — h2-only ⇒ blind relay (origin's real cert reaches the
  client), `[h2, http/1.1]` ⇒ MITM as today, fragmented/malformed/ECH-like ⇒ fail-open to MITM, and the
  peek never exceeds the shared handshake deadline. grpc-go ≥1.67 completes through the bypass.
- **Regression:** `pinned`/`opaque`/`error` still publish exactly once per connection, never silently,
  always with `Reason`. The log write→read-through-sanitise round trip (§6.1) is rebuilt.
- Gate: `-race` ×2, both builds, `architext validate`, coverage ≥ 80%.

## 8. Risks

- **Masking regression is the top risk**, and §1.4 proved it is not theoretical: bounded capture already
  defeated the body-secret rule once, in shipped code. Every partial-publish path must mask what it has,
  and the truncation-boundary test is mandatory, not optional.
- **Framing complexity is the second.** The correlation queue + custom skip reader + trailer handling +
  half-close/linger semantics is real HTTP-proxy machinery replacing a byte sniffer. The mitigation is
  that every failure has one honest destination (`opaque`, once, with `Reason`) rather than a resync
  heuristic.
- **The merge's join is third.** Two rounds found two different join bugs (destination namespaces, then
  name provenance). The path-keyed design is verified against both; the §7 join tests (long names,
  fallback counting) exist because this seam has now failed twice.
- **Wire-format churn:** socket, rotating log, reader and `sanitizeMessage` all change at once; old logs
  get exactly one loud version error (§3.1.7) — neither silence nor a crash-loop of notices.
- **Scope creep into Exfiltration:** the merge touches a large working subsystem, and rounds 2–3 *added*
  scope to it (path-keyed join, self-exclusion precision, log fallback, drop/eviction honesty). That
  scope is the price of the merge telling the truth; it does not extend to redesigning the view itself.
- **This reworks shipped, verified code.** v0.7.0 is cuttable today. Phase 2.5 lands on its own branch
  off `develop` **after** that cut, not by holding the release open.
